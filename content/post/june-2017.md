June 10, 2017 was the last day I wrote a non-review post on this site.

The article, [How to Backup SNES Saves With the Super UFO Pro 8](/post/super-ufo-pro-8-backup-saves), was what I remember doing back when I was first on the internet, write down what I knew in the hopes that it would help someone else.

Three more pages back in time, we've got November 19, 2016, and [How to Copy a PICO-8 Game From a PocketCHIP](/post/how-to-copy-a-pico-8-game-from-a-pocketchip/).

January 9, 2016, [Setting up a Raspberry Pi 2 Samba server (in early January 2016)](/post/setting-up-a-raspberry-pi-2-samba-server-in-early-january-2016/), followed by [Google Services on the Amazon Fire HD 8](/post/google-services-on-the-amazon-fire-hd-8/) on December 13, 2015.

June 20, 2015 we've got [Example libGDX orthographic camera controller](/post/example-libgdx-orthographic-camera-controller/).

All this isn't to say that my online activity has stopped.

[My Stack Overflow profile](https://stackoverflow.com/users/11912/james-skemp), while slowing over the last few years, shows I was active out there over the last 18 years and two weeks.

Out on [GitHub](https://github.com/JamesSkemp), while joining back in 2011, I started to really share my work there starting in 2013, only really slowing down when I started [my own Gitea instance](https://git.ebacher-skemp.com/explore/repos) and pushing mostly over there.

[Trygve Lode's The Treehouse](https://www.trygve.com/) is a site I'll randomly think of and pull up. [The Visible Barbie Project](https://www.trygve.com/visible_barbie.html) is what I remember leading me to this site many decades ago. It's still got one of my comments on the site.

[The last post on his blog](https://www.trygve.com/blog.html), August 4th, 2012. I've seen those robins so many times over the last 14 years.

I can't find it anymore, but I can recall a brief exchange with someone who posted his diary on some random website. We exchanged messages back and forth. He cataloged his dreams, I think. Maybe that sites still around somewhere.

What happened to Trygve? The site's still up, so the domain and hosting is still being covered. Somewhere I've got a backup of the site, because to me it's still a visible sign of the old internet.

Before weird people decided how they thought the web should be. How people should communicate with each other. How you should make friends. How you should keep in touch with them.

Maybe it's because I'm getting older. But the signposts were there. Cause and effect is the one thing I've believed in the longest. If you don't know why something happened it just means you don't have enough information about the system that the thing happened in.

June 10, 2017 was the last day I wrote a non-review post on this site.

- 2017: 15 reviews
- 2018: 10 reviews
- 2019: 4 reviews
- 2020: nothing
- 2021: 1 review
- 2022-2025: nothing
- 2026: 1 review

I'm still part of Amazon Vine, but in 2019, due to many contributing factors, I stopped caring. I had a young child, job changes, things were busy.

2021 was [a game I really enjoyed and wanted to make sure people knew about it](https://words.strivinglife.com/post/review-draconis-invasion-wrath/).

2026 was because the helpful person on support asked me to write a review if he provided good service.

Every few weeks I add some content to [my Git Commands book](https://git.jamesrskemp.com/). It's the same content, with just some version numbers changing. Every once in a while there's something special I want to make note of.

Is the vast majority of the updates on that useful to anyone? No. :) But I still do it.

It's a "Hey, I'm good with what I do in Git, and most of the time it's the same basic stuff. But I'm still alive. This site has been kicking since 2018, and it's still going."

Supposedly the oldest post on here is from 1999. That's not true. [First WordPress post](/post/first-wordpress-post/) is from April 30, 2005. In one of my Git repos I've got the StrivingLife.net network graphics that I created, [when I thought that was going to be a thing](/post/an-idea-has-formed-what-this-will-be-for/). (But don't think I meant I was going to be an influencer - that word meant nothing 20 years ago. People didn't have businesses that ran blogs, or studios setup. Someone reaching out, via email, after reading something you wrote, once every few weeks was noteworthy.)

So ... I'm still kicking. I'm still thinking about the path I moved along, the people I meet, the experiences I had. Just not thinking about them as much.

And all this because I've been thinking about posting somewhere about 3D printing, and whether I put it on this dusty site, or create another book site to plop it on. Or another blog entirely. Heh. At one point I had even thought about putting my reviews off on their own thing. But at that time you'd need a database and some web software even if you were just going to create a static site. And you'd have to pay for a domain, and a place to store it ...

But you also wouldn't have to worry about some large business crawling your written content so they train a model and sell it as a subscription, like a larger and larger chunk of the internet these days.

If you read this far, I hope you're doing well. And I hope this helps you, even if only to know that you too could write something that someone else one day reads. Even if it's just your future self. My email should be in the footer, if you want to say hi.

And if no one reads it, even my busy future self, that's okay too. Because it had been about nine years, 3 months, and 20 days since I last wrote a non-review post. And now, just maybe, I've got some inspiration to not let that happen again.
