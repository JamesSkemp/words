## Quick to assemble, sturdy, and top-tier support

My mom is temporary disabled due to a fracture in her leg and is unable to put any weight on her foot.

She's been using a standard walker but was looking to get something that would allow her to do meal prep in her small kitchen (by sitting down) and then easily move the food into her living room.

HOMLAND's Rollators were recommended to her by a family member as something that had worked well for them.

The item was packaged well, but was missing all the knobs and screws. When I reached out to HOMLAND support for replacement parts, Aaron, who was extremely friendly and easy to talk to, quickly noted the parts I needed and got them shipped out soon after, with a tracking number sent to me. Aaron also followed up after the replacements were delivered to make sure I received them.

Once I got everything I needed, assembly went fairly well, with some of the wheels being a little tricky to keep in one place, but less than 10 minutes from start to finish.

What worked well for us was securing one side of the Rollator's wheels and handles and then doing a quick test to see how well the heights worked. This allowed her to have her walker close. Then I secured all the wheels at the same height, left one of the handles unscrewed so she could do a better test of the correct height (also nicely noted and pictured in the instructions that were included). Once we had the ideal handle height it was easy to screw them both in.

My mom had no issues using the brakes, including the locking ones (which I thought might be a little tough). She felt safe sitting with the brakes engaged and was able to get back up without issue; with the brakes engaged it was quite sturdy. The width of the Rollator is the same size as her standard walker. The depth is about 1.5 times as large, but the turning radius of the wheels meant she was still able to quickly move around her apartment.

So far, I can see why a family member recommended my mom get [HOMLAND's Rollator](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F42QR3PK?th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=strivinglifen-20&linkId=2f8fc54e1993e7281871c25a463b4b5d&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=false&optOutTS=1785196026342&ref_=as_li_ss_tl). I don't know that she could have skipped a standard walker and gone right to this after her accident (was hit by a car while walking, so was bruised, weak, and in a lot of pain), but it definitely looks like it'll work for her as a good transition from walker to walking.
